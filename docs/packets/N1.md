# N1: Make a finished transition cheap

Read `AGENTS.md` first, then this file. It is your whole task.

Owns: `src/SA.Motion/Inertialization/PoseTransition.cs`, `tests/SA.Motion.Tests/TransitionEarlyOutTests.cs`. Edit nothing else.

## Why

Measured under Burst (single thread, i9-14900K): `PoseTransition.Apply` costs 43-47 ns at unit scale and 76-81 ns otherwise. Most of the unit-scale cost is the rotation `Quaternion.AxisAngle` (a double-precision sin and cos), which still runs after the transition has finished or when the rotation correction was cleared.

## Change

- `VectorOffset.Apply`, `RotationOffset.Apply` and `PoseTransition.Apply` keep their signatures.
- When `Decay.Duration <= Scalar.DurationEpsilon` or `elapsed >= Decay.Duration`, a channel returns its destination without evaluating the polynomial, `AxisAngle` or `Multiply`. The rotation channel returns `Quaternion.Normalize(destination)`, which is what the full formula yields today.
- Struct fields and layout do not change (Unity tests assert `Pose` 40 bytes, `ScalarDecay` 28, `PoseHistory` 88).
- Results for unfinished transitions must not change at all.

## Tests (new file)

- A reference implementation of the old expression inside the test file: `Normalize(Multiply(AxisAngle(Axis, Decay.Evaluate(elapsed).Value), Normalize(destination)))` and `destination + Direction * Decay.Evaluate(elapsed).Value`.
- Compare new `Apply` to the reference over a grid of `elapsed` from -1 to 2 x Duration, for 200 deterministic random offsets, within 1e-6. Include `elapsed == Duration` exactly, `elapsed > Duration`, default (cleared) offsets, and NaN `elapsed`.
- `PoseTransition.Apply` on a fully finished transition returns the destination pose exactly (position, normalized rotation, scale).
