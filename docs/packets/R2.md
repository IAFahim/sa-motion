## Deliver

`BalanceTick/BalanceController.cs` with one entry point:

```text
static BalanceReport Tick(in BalanceParams parameters, ref BalanceControllerState state, ReadOnlySpan<RoleBody> bodies, ReadOnlySpan<SupportProbe> feet, in BalanceInput input, Span<JointDrive> drives)
```

`feet` has two entries (left, right). `drives` has `Roles.Count` entries indexed by role; write only the roles the balance controller owns (pelvis, torso, legs) and leave the others untouched. `BalanceInput` carries the timestep, the desired stance and an animation-derived target pose per leg if you need one; define it in your folder. `BalanceReport` says: supported or not, which foot is stepping, a step in progress, and a decision (`Standing`, `Stepping`, `Falling`). `BalanceControllerState` holds every carried field (countdowns, previous targets, hysteresis).

## Check, using the harness (`tests/SA.Harness`, `docs/harness.md`; add a project reference)

Run at 60 Hz with `HarnessWorld.ApplyDrives(drives)` each tick. Record the measured numbers in `docs/handoff/R2.md` and say plainly which targets were met.

1. Stand: no push, 10 s. Target: pelvis stays within 0.12 m of its start height, torso within 15 degrees of upright.
2. Small push: 60 N·s at the torso in each of eight horizontal directions. Target: no fall after 5 s, and for the larger of those pushes at least one foot steps more than 0.1 m on the push side.
3. Large push: 400 N·s. Target: the report says `Falling` and stays so.
4. Determinism: two identical runs give identical drives.
5. Hostile input (NaN, infinity) gives passive drives and a finite report.

If a target is not met, do not weaken it or the harness. Report the measured values, the likely limiting factor (harness torque, gains, port adaptation) and what you tried.
