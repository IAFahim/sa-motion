How a caller drives a layered evaluation (this is what the test's `PoseScene.Blend` does): start `pose` from the rest pose and keep one `PoseSum[]` scratch the size of the rig. For `layer = NextLayer(states, -1)` while `layer >= 0`, `layer = NextLayer(states, layer)`: call `Override` once per override state of that layer, then `Resolve(pose, sums)`, then `Additive` once per additive state of that layer. The `weight` argument is the state's `Weight * Fade`, `frame` is `Phase.Frame(state.Phase, state.Wrap, frameCount)`, and `mask` is the per-bone weights of the state's mask. Checking that the clip and mask belong to the rig (the `Contributes` part of Unity's `Evaluation`) stays with the caller.

## Decisions that differ from or add to the packet text

1. `Resolve` takes `Span<PoseSum> sums`, not `ReadOnlySpan<PoseSum>`. The packet lists the read-only type but also says `Resolve` clears `sums`, which needs a writable span. A caller holding a `Span<PoseSum>` compiles against either.
2. `frameCount` fixes the layout of `samples`: the clip's bone count is `samples.Length / frameCount` (integer division, 0 when `frameCount <= 0`) and samples after the last whole frame are ignored. `sums`, `pose`, `reference` and `mask` may be longer or shorter than that; every kernel visits only the bones all of them have (the shortest wins). `Resolve` visits `min(pose, sums)` and then clears all of `sums`.
3. A state only contributes when `weight` is positive and finite, and a bone only when `mask[b] * weight` is positive and finite. That is Unity's `Contributes` (`Weight * Fade > 0`) plus its per-bone `weight > 0`. Zero, negative, NaN and infinite weights are skipped without touching the output.
4. Totality policy (so "never NaN" holds for every input, not only NaN):
   - A NaN or infinite blend factor or weight inside `PoseMath` acts as 0 (`Lerp(a, b, NaN)` is `a`; `Add` with a NaN weight keeps the sum).
   - A NaN frame or `-Infinity` samples frame 0; `+Infinity` and anything past the end sample the last frame. A bad `boneCount`, a bad `bone`, an empty span, or fewer samples than one frame give `Pose.Identity`.
   - A result field that is not finite (NaN input, infinity, or overflow such as `float.MaxValue` times 2) is replaced by its neutral value: position zero, rotation identity, scale one; scale zero for a delta or a sum; the zero quaternion for a sum's rotation; zero for a matrix column. Rotations go through `Quaternion.Normalize`, which already maps non-finite or near-zero input to identity.
   - Finite, ordinary inputs take exactly the Unity arithmetic; the guards only change a result that would not have been finite.
   - Not guarded on purpose: an entry of `pose` or `sums` that no state touches is left as the caller passed it, so a non-finite entry the caller put there stays. Entries a call does write are always finite.
5. `Mean` of an empty sum is position zero, rotation identity, scale zero, exactly as in Unity (`inverse` is 0 when the weight is not positive). `Resolve` never asks for it. A positive weight too small to invert (below about 3e-39) overflows the reciprocal, and the guards then give exactly `Pose.Identity` (tested with a weight of 1e-40).
6. `Hierarchy.Model` writes `min(parents, local, model)` bones and leaves the rest of `model` alone. A parent that is negative, equal to or greater than the bone index (so also anything out of range) makes the bone a root.
7. Safe-normalize threshold: `SA.Motion.Numerics.Quaternion.Normalize` returns identity when the squared length is at most 1e-12. Unity's `normalizesafe` uses a threshold of its own that I did not re-read for this packet, so the two can differ for quaternions shorter than about 1e-6 and for nothing else I know of.
8. `Affine` is a plain struct with public fields (like `Float3`, `Quaternion`, `Pose`), not a readonly struct, so adapters can fill it field by field.
9. `PoseMath.Positive`, `ClipSampling.Stride`, `ClipSampling.Locate` and `ClipSampling.Sample` are `internal`: they are the single place `At` and `PoseBlend` share the frame location and the sample blend, so the two cannot drift apart. The consequence for tests is that comparing `PoseBlend` with `At` cannot see a bug in that shared code, so `At` is also pinned directly with uneven fractions.

## Tests (`tests/SA.Animation.Tests/PoseTests.cs`)

- Ported from `CoreTests.cs`: lerp endpoints, accumulating a delta recovers the pose, the mean of one ignores the quaternion sign, affine against `System.Numerics.Matrix4x4` composition (50 random pairs, with the row/column transpose mapped), sampling hits frames and interpolates, and the evaluation tests that need no `Playback` (empty states give the rest pose, single clip and half weight, an override layer stacks on the layer below, a zero mask leaves the layer below, additive from its reference on a non-identity rest, evaluation ignores state order). The `Playback`-driven Unity tests belong to packet A2.
- Parity (the packet's test): 200 random state lists from `System.Random(7)`, 2 to 6 states, layers 0 to 2, both modes, random masks, 3 bones, 5 frames, with a fresh random world per run. `PoseBlend` driven layer by layer is compared per component (rotation components directly, no sign alignment) at 1e-5 against a per-bone copy of `Evaluation.Bone` in the test file. The test also asserts that the lists were not trivial: over the 200 runs 149 had a contributing override, 159 a contributing additive, 111 both, 33 two or more overrides stacked in one layer, and 196 moved a bone more than 1e-3 off the rest pose. A second parity test uses 200 lists of 4 to 8 always-contributing states on two layers, so stacked means, sign anchoring and additive chains run every time.
- Totality: five fuzz tests (20,000 iterations each, run once with a palette of NaN, infinities, `float.Epsilon`, `float.MaxValue`, 1e30 and so on, and once with finite extremes only) over `PoseMath`, `ClipSampling.At`, `PoseBlend`, hostile `sums` that a call touches, and `Hierarchy.Model`: no throw and every output component finite. Also: non-finite weights, frames and clip data leave the rest pose alone or give finite poses; `NextLayer` accepts any starting layer including `int.MinValue` and `int.MaxValue`; a non-finite blend factor acts as 0; each guarded field falls back to its documented neutral value.
- Layout and cost: `Marshal.SizeOf<Affine>() == 48` with the four offsets; an allocation test runs every kernel 1,000 times after warm-up and asserts 0 bytes with `GC.GetAllocatedBytesForCurrentThread()`.
- Hierarchy: a 3-bone chain with a rotated, non-uniformly scaled middle bone against hand-composed columns; random 12-bone trees (20 runs) against `Matrix4x4`; bad parents (negative, self, forward, `int.MaxValue`, `int.MinValue`, out of range) become roots; span-length mismatches.

## Do the tests catch a wrong kernel

Beyond "green", I applied single-line mutations to the five source files, one at a time (edit, build, run the suite on the net10.0 library build, restore with `git checkout`), and counted how many the suite noticed. The scripts live in a scratch directory, not in the repository. The mutations covered: wrong operand order in `Delta`, `Accumulate` and `Mul`; dropped sign alignment, weight cap, early return, `Clear` or parent translation; swapped `first`/`next`; an inverted frame blend; each parent rule; each neutral fallback constant; each guard removed; and similar.

- First sweep, 30 mutants: 28 killed, 2 survived. `Add` without sign alignment survived because the ported Unity test compares rotations without sign (so `q` and `-q` look equal); a new test flips input signs and demands the same mean. `1 / weight` without its inner guard survived because the output guards already cover it, so that guard was dead code and is removed.
- Second sweep, 17 mutants (the neutral fallback constants, `Mul`, `Align` and `Affine` unguarded, four sampling mutants): 16 killed, 1 survived. An inverted frame blend survived because the shared sampling helper is exercised by both sides of the parity test and the direct asserts used only symmetric fractions (0.5, 1.5). A new test uses 0.25, 0.75, 1.25, 1.75.
- Third sweep, 4 mutants (the survivor plus three swapped `first`/`next` variants): all killed.
- 49 distinct mutants in total: 48 killed, 1 equivalent (removed).
- After the fixes, all three sweeps were run against commit `596af65` (source identical to the final commit; the only later change is one more test, `AWeightTooSmallToInvertGivesTheNeutralPose`, and added tests cannot un-kill a mutant): sweep 1 killed 29 of 29 applicable mutants (the 30th, the dead `1 / weight` guard, no longer exists), sweep 2 killed 17 of 17, sweep 3 killed 4 of 4. The worktree was clean (`git diff` empty) after every sweep.
- Not covered by this method: anything the mutants did not touch, and mutants that need two coordinated edits. It shows the tests are sensitive to these kernel mistakes, not that the kernels are free of faults.
