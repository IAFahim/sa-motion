# Handoff L4: leg solver at least twice as fast

## Result

Target met. On this machine (Intel Core i9-14900K, Linux x64, .NET SDK 10.0.401, runtime 10.0.12) `dotnet run -c Release --project tests/SA.Motion.Benchmarks` reports 242 to 244 ns per leg. The unchanged solver reports 678 to 694 ns with the same benchmark. That is 2.8 times faster and about 57 ns under the 300 ns target. `tests/SA.Motion.Tests/LegTests.cs` and every other existing test pass unedited on both library builds.

Edited: `src/SA.Motion/Legs/LegRig.cs`, `Fulcrum.cs`, `SoftReach.cs`, `Planting.cs`, `src/SA.Motion/Mechanics/CriticalSpring.cs`. New: `tests/SA.Motion.Benchmarks/` (console project, not in `SA.Motion.sln`, no packages). No public signature changed.

The packet's 640 ns is explained by the JIT, not by the solver. An early version of this benchmark let the JIT inline `LegRig.Step` into the benchmark loop through on-stack replacement and read 629 to 639 for roughly 25 rounds. After the loop was recompiled at the final tier the same solver read 679 to 698. The committed benchmark marks its loop `AggressiveOptimization` so `Step` stays a real call, and warms up for 4 s so tiering has finished, which makes the number stable (spread about 3 to 4 percent over five runs). The scenario also reproduces: 59 percent of legs planted, 3.7 percent plant or release events per frame.

Run-to-run note: the same source built in two different directories gave 316 and 338 in a scratch experiment, so differences under about 5 ns per leg between builds are not resolvable. Every kept change below was measured on at least three runs.

The packet's ideas, one by one:

- `MathF` instead of `Math` with double round trips: `MathF.Sqrt` in the hot normalizations is worth about 43 ns per leg (ablation: putting `(float)Math.Sqrt` back at those five sites gives 281). It returns exactly the same value as the double route, so results are bit identical. `MathF.Exp`, `Asin` and `Cos` gave nothing (236 to 258 depending on the variant, against 239 to 249 for the original calls) and were not adopted, which keeps the double precision libm results.
- skip a spring step at rest: -7.
- `cos(MaxWalkableSlope)` once per `Step`: -3.
- skip `PoseTransition.Apply` when the blend has finished: neutral when first added (390 before and after), but a final A/B shows 253 without it and 237 with it, so it is kept (-15).
- avoid repeated `Normalize` and `Rotate`: the ankle algebra, the arm reuse in `Plan`, no second normalization of the animated foot and the known bone length in `Turn` account for about -82 together.
- skip slope alignment when the normal equals up: part of the -49 line above.

Tried and not kept, with numbers: sharing the unit ground normal between the walkable test and `Plan` (272 to 281 against 272 to 276, reverted); skipping the body scale multiply when the scale is 1 (272 to 274 against 266 to 268, reverted); calling `Valid()` again in `Resolve` instead of reading the planned state (242 to 261 against 236 to 239); `AggressiveOptimization` on the leg methods (417, it loses PGO); `AggressiveInlining` on every `Float3` and `Quaternion` operator in a scratch copy (those files are not owned): 262 on the default JIT but 338 with `TieredPGO=0`. A first `Turn` that normalized by the analytic norm was 241 but its quaternion error near a folded leg reached 8.6e-3 where the original had 3e-6; it normalizes by the real norm now.

## Why NoInlining

The JIT inlines the `Float3` and `Quaternion` operators into a root method only until a budget tied to that root's IL size runs out. `Step` absorbed `Resolve` and the solver and spent the budget early, so hot operators stayed calls (a scratch run that forced every operator inline gained 50 to 70 ns). Marking the three large steps `NoInlining` gives each its own budget. The attribute does not change results. Its effect on Mono and IL2CPP is not measured.

## Equivalence

Behavior is the same up to float rounding. Evidence, all from runs:

- `Prepared` is bit identical to `Scaled(Sanitize(p))`: 3.5 million leg-frames with random, corrupt and out-of-range parameters and body scales, zero difference and an identical benchmark checksum at that commit.
- A scratch differential test (not committed, it embeds copies of the original sources) runs the original and the new solver in lock step on identical inputs. Final run: 3 seeds of 100,000 random scenarios of 60 frames, 39.0 million leg-frames, with NaN, infinite and out-of-range parameters, invalid inputs, tilted ground, ground steps, authored contacts, bad up and forward vectors, and 14 kinds of corrupted leg state injected. Zero scenarios diverged in plant state or solved flag. Largest differences: ankle 6.7e-6 m, contact 6.7e-6 m, foot quaternion 2.8e-6, pelvis 5.3e-6 m, pelvis shift 1.8e-7 m, stretch 3.6e-7, pin anchor 8.3e-7 m. Blend durations can differ for sub-micron offsets with no visible effect.
- Upper and lower rotation quaternions differ by up to 1.1e-4 in nearly straight or fully folded legs, where the two bone problem is ill conditioned. Against a double precision port of the original math over 3 million well conditioned cases the maximum quaternion error is 9.16e-4 for the new solver and 9.13e-4 for the original, ankle error 1.55e-7 for both, so the new solver is as accurate as the old one.

## Limits and not done

- Only the default JIT meets 300. With `TieredPGO=0` or `TieredCompilation=0` the solver is about 2.1 times faster than before (438 to 448 against 930 to 935) but stays above 300. Closing that gap needs `AggressiveInlining` on the operators in `Float3.cs` and `Quaternion.cs`, which this packet does not own, and it costs the default JIT about 25 ns.
- Ground with a slope costs about 314 ns per leg, 2.3 times faster than before but 80 ns above flat ground. The extra cost sits in the slope path: `Fulcrum.Tilted` still goes through `TwoBoneIK.FromTo`, `Canonical`, `Atan2` and `AxisAngle`. Not optimized and not profiled further, because the packet scenario is flat ground.
- `Prepared` lists all 26 `LegParams` fields, so it duplicates the field list of `LegParamsSpec` (the bounds still come from `LegParamsSpec.Bounds`). A constant that divides by zero when `LegParamsSpec.Count` differs from 26 fails the build if someone adds a field without updating it. A test that compares it with `Scaled(Sanitize(p))` would need an `internal` hook or a new test file, both outside this packet.
- Where the time goes now (perf at 251 ns, percent of samples, of which about 14 percent is the benchmark generating inputs): `SoftReach.Reach` 20, `Resolve` 18, `Plan` 12, `Step` 9, `Prepared` and `Bounds` 7, springs 3, `PoseTransition` 3. Removing `Prepared` entirely was worth 32 ns per leg in an ablation, which is the next candidate if more is needed.
- Not measured: Unity (pure `src/` packet, `eng/unity.sh` never run), Mono or IL2CPP speed, Windows or macOS, multi-threaded runs, characters with more than two legs.
